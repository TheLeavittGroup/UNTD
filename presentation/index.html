Stage 1

Diagnose before pitching.

Make the homeowner feel understood first. The later close should sound like a recap of what they already told you, not a random pitch.

01

Frame the visit

“I’m going to understand the home, bill, and goals first. Then I’ll show whether solar actually makes sense.”

02

Find pain

Rate hikes, summer bills, fixed income, AC usage, EV charging, outages, or previous bad quotes.

03

Build the gap

Compare what they do now against what they want: lower cost, control, backup, predictability, and ownership.

04

Earn permission

“If the house qualifies and the numbers are clearly better, is there any reason we wouldn’t reserve it tonight?”

Stage 3

Present the plan like a premium product.

Each slide is designed to keep the rep in control while keeping the homeowner focused on what matters.

Current reality

Your utility bill is not a bill. It is a subscription that keeps repricing itself.

Set the contrast: right now the homeowner rents power from the utility with no control over future pricing. Solar turns that uncertainty into a structured home-energy plan.

“The problem is not only what you paid last month. The problem is that you have no control over what they charge you later.”

Custom design

This is built around usage, roof, shade, utility rules, and goals.

Explain system size, roof planes, battery logic, production guarantee, warranty, installer credibility, and what happens if usage changes.

“I don’t need you to love solar. I need the design to solve the exact problem you said you wanted solved.”

Protection stack

The real close is confidence: warranty, install process, monitoring, and support.

Remove risk by covering workmanship, roof penetrations, production monitoring, inspections, permission to operate, and the rep’s role after signature.

“The panels are only part of this. What matters is the install, the guarantee, and who still answers the phone after today.”

Stage 5

Close without sounding pushy.

Objections get answered by returning to the homeowner’s own words and the math they already saw.

“I need to think about it.”

“That makes sense. Usually that means either the numbers, the trust, or the timing is not clear yet. Which one is it for you?”

“I want to compare quotes.”

“You should compare. Just compare payment, escalator, production, battery, installer, warranty, and what the utility bill looks like after solar.”

“What if I move?”

“Then the buyer gets a home with a lower operating cost. The real question is whether you would rather keep renting power while you are here.”

“I don’t like contracts.”

“Totally fair. Your utility is already a contract with no price protection. This is deciding whether you want control over the terms.”
