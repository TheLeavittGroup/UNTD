The IID Pain Points

Scheduled Base-Rate Changes

22.30¢/kWh is IID’s listed 2026 residential base energy rate, with 24.38¢ approved for 2027. IID restructured rates and eliminated the former ECA, so actual bills still depend on usage and other charges.

IID Rate Update

New Solar Is Not 1:1 NEM

IID’s legacy Net Energy Metering program is closed to new applications. New systems use Net Billing: grid purchases are billed at the customer rate while exports receive a separate credit.

IID Rate Schedules

Lower Export Credit

6.98¢/kWh is IID’s currently listed compensation for excess solar sent to the grid. IID says the credit is variable and tied to its lowest wholesale solar contract cost.

IID Net Billing

Extreme Summer HVAC Load

IID itself warns that summer consumption is very high in its service area because of air-conditioning demand. That makes the hottest months the hardest bills to control.

IID Solar FAQs

100% Historical-Use Limit

IID says a solar system may be sized only up to 100% of the property’s previous 12 months of electricity use, measured in kWh.

IID Sizing Rules

TOU Is Not Open to Everyone

IID says legacy net-metering customers do not qualify for its residential Shift & Save Time-of-Use rate. Net-billing eligibility should be confirmed directly with IID for the specific account.

IID TOU Eligibility

Why a Battery Matters in IID

Keep More of Your Solar

Store daytime production for later instead of exporting as much energy at the lower net-billing credit.

Buy Less After Sunset

Use stored solar in the evening and overnight, when the panels are no longer producing.

Design Around Summer

Size solar and storage around the home’s real 12-month usage and heavy cooling load—not a generic estimate.

Optional Backup Power

A properly designed and configured battery system can keep selected circuits running during an outage.
